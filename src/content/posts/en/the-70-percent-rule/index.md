If someone else can do a task 70% as well as you, give it to them.

Most people refuse. They look at the 30% gap and decide it is faster to do it themselves. Today, they are right. Over a quarter, they are wrong.

## What the 30% really costs

- **Your time.** Every hour on a task someone else could own is an hour off the work only you can do.
- **Their growth.** 70% today becomes 90% in a few months, but only if they get the reps.
- **Your ceiling.** If everything runs through you, you are the bottleneck. The team moves as fast as your calendar.

The gap is also smaller than it looks. Half of your "extra 30%" is polish nobody notices. The other half is context you never wrote down.

## 70% is a number Bezos likes too

In his [2016 shareholder letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders), Jeff Bezos wrote that most decisions should be made with around 70% of the information you wish you had. Wait for 90% and you are probably being slow.

Same logic, different problem. Waiting for perfect information and waiting for a perfect delegate both feel responsible. Both quietly cost you speed.

## Delegate what you know best

Andy Grove makes a counterintuitive point in *High Output Management*: delegate the tasks you know best, not the ones you understand least. If you know the work, you can tell quickly whether the 70% is good enough, and coach the gap.

He also adds the catch:

> Delegation without follow-through is abdication.

The 70% rule is not "hand it off and disappear." It is hand it off, check the outcome, and close the gap together. Early on, you check often. As the 70% climbs, you check less.

## How to start

Pick one recurring task this week that someone could do at 70%. Write down the context that lives in your head. Hand it over. Review the result, not the steps.

Perfect is the enemy of delegated.
